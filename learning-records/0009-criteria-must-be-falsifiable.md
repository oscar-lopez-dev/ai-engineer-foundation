# Criteria must be falsifiable

Lesson 0006 (Stage B1) is delivered: a criterion counts only if someone could have failed it. The gate question is "what would I observe if this were false?", with three repairs — measure the adjective, demote the unanswerable to a question, drop the one that describes construction. Robertson & Robertson's *fit criterion* is the anchor term, now in the glossary.

The non-obvious part worth protecting: an unclear outcome is a **question**, not a weak criterion (Wynne). A colleague asks when they hit one; an agent resolves it silently and reports success. And the opposite failure is real — exhaustive scenarios produce Adzic's "illusion of precision," so key examples at boundaries are the target, not coverage.

**Evidence:** lesson shipped with a five-item falsifiability check; no learner output yet. Treat as exposure until they bring real ticket criteria through the gate. Per the new teaching split, practice happens on their own repos, so verification will come from what they paste back rather than from in-lesson work.

**Implications:** B1 is the vocabulary base for B4 ("safe to ship") — review means pointing at a criterion that failed. B2 (bounding the problem) should come next so criteria are written about something already bounded. Do not teach Gherkin syntax, requirement taxonomies, or Volere template mechanics as skills.
